## Overview

Vehicle platoons must keep their formation while responding to changes in the leader's motion and disturbances from the road. String stability describes the ability to prevent those disturbances from growing as they travel through the platoon.

This work develops a distributed observer–controller co-design framework. Each vehicle estimates the platoon's formation-tracking errors using locally measured outputs and information exchanged with neighboring vehicles. The controller uses these estimates to generate its control signal without explicitly calculating the desired spacing locally.

## Method at a glance

### Platoon-level state estimation

Distributed observers reconstruct the tracking errors of all follower vehicles relative to a global desired trajectory. These estimated errors provide the feedback needed for formation control.

### Reconstruction of private control inputs

Each observer reconstructs other vehicles' control inputs from its own state estimates, avoiding the need to communicate those private input signals.

### Joint observer and controller synthesis

A weighted H∞ performance index combines string stability with observer estimation robustness. Young's inequality decouples observer–controller coupling terms, yielding sufficient LMI conditions for simultaneous design through convex optimization.

## Validation in QLabs

The study uses four QCar2 digital twins—one leader and three followers—in Quanser Interactive Labs. The tested scenarios cover constant-speed cruising on a smooth road, leader start–stop maneuvers, and traversal of a wavy road. The setup uses a minimum spacing of 0.8 m, a time headway of 1 s, and a communication update rate of 20 Hz.

The vehicle-platoon results presented here are high-fidelity simulations. The manuscript also reports checks of individual system components on physical QCar2 platforms; a physical multi-vehicle platooning experiment is left for future work.
